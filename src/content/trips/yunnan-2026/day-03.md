We got breakfast right outside our stay and headed straight for Dali Train Station. 

![](media/day-03/breakfast.webp)

Behind the wheel of the worn, manual cab we took sat a woman well past retirement age, yet her control of the vehicle was nothing short of graceful - unlike the jerky, reckless driving of younger drivers. Even as she rushed to get us to the train station on time (we were kinda running late), the ride never once felt frantic. She was even helpful enough to enter the carpark for us to have an easier access to the platform.  
We almost decided to reschedule the train ticket but fortunately we were able to rush through the security checks with some time to spare.

After a 3 hour ride, we arrived at Shangrila. Right outside the train station was a huge parade square, with shops lining the sides of it. 

![](media/day-03/shangrila-square.webp)

We grabbed lunch at one of the stalls. Unfortunately their yak milk was out.

![](media/day-03/cooking.mp4)
![](media/day-03/fried-rice.webp)

Our hotel was located right at the edge of Dukezhong Ancient Town (独克宗古镇). We went to roam the streets after settling down and resting a bit in our room. 

![](media/day-03/hotel-view-2.webp "View of the ancient town from the hotel.")

The main attraction we visited that day was Guishan Park (龟山公园), which was within the ancient town itself. We pretty much walked a quarter of the main city that day.

Along the way we got some treats and for dinner we ate yak hot pot. Meat was kinda tough, kinda got scammed into buying a plate of morchella mushrooms (they advertised as though it was limited to Shangrila but I realised I’ve eaten these in Singapore). 

![](media/day-03/dinner.webp)

Their yak beancurd was surprisingly tasty with tsampa sprinkled on top. Must try. I regret not having another bowl during my time there. On the way back to our lodge, there was a dance festival right in front of Guishan Park. Zero clue what was going on but it was quite a lively atmosphere.
