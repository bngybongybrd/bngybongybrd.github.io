The day started off with a home cooked breakfast in the hotel, where we chatted with the hosts and talked to them about our itinerary. They suggested numerous more places to hike and provided some advice. 

![](media/day-04/breakfast.webp)

After breakfast, we headed off on a walk to the bus station to purchase our tickets to Ninong, the starting point of our hike up to Yubeng. Online purchases were kinda restricted to locals so we had to go down physically.
After our purchase, we decided to head to Songzanlin Monastery which was close by.
The whole area around the Monastery required a ticket for entry, so we had to get tickets and then take a shuttle bus up to the Monastery’s entrance.
Upon reaching, I realised I could faintly remember my visit ten years ago. Regardless, we headed in after taking some pictures.

![](media/day-04/monastery-entrance.webp)
![](media/day-04/monastery-stairs.webp)

Right after passing the entrance gate, there were several lingering drivers asking tourists if they needed a ride up to the monastery. We ignored them and continued our way - no point tryna take a short cut if it dulls our experience. There were a few flights of stairs to climb, but it proved manageable.

At the top, there were a few temples. Unlike museums, these are actual religious places where around 700 monks live and study. At the time of visiting one of the smaller prayer halls, I could hear them chanting in unison.

After touring the Monastery, we headed out and got on a bus, hoping it would bring us to the next scenic area. Unfortunately it was a public bus that brought us out of the entire area and toward the main town.
We decided to drop off at the town to get something to eat.

![](media/day-04/snacks.webp "Yak meat skewers")

Since we had quite some time to spare, we decided to rent an electric scooter and head out to Heidou cafe, a popular destination overlooking Napa Lake. I was pretty excited to try out riding the scooter. Interestingly, in China, the minimum age to ride an electric scooter is 16, and no licence is required. The first vendor denied renting to us since we told them we didnt have any riding experience, which was responsible of him, though we almost cancelled our plans due to that. Fortunately, we found another vendor that was willing to rent to us, since he assumed we were from Malaysia, and told us it was similar to riding scooters there, after I asked if it was easy to ride. The scooter we rented had a speed limit of 25km/h on its speedometer, but according to our GPS, its maximum was 48. Riding to Heidou cafe was fun in and of itself since it was my first. Even with Quentin as a pillion rider, getting familiarised with riding wasn't that big of an issue.

At Heidou cafe, the entrance fee was to purchase something on the menu for each customer. We both got a drink and headed in.

After dinner, we returned the scooter, and on the way back, there was another dance festival at a small square within the ancient town. We didn't stay for long and headed back to rest.
