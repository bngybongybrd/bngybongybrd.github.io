We started the hike up around 1520. The initial hike up a small hill had already started to prove challenging as I was panting at the top, before even purchasing entrance tickets. We managed to get ourselves a student price at half the original price.

The first portion of the trail was relatively flat. We had to trace along the outer edge of the mountains before cutting inward through the mountain gorge. From then onwards, the climb started to get steeper. It was easily one of the most strenuous physical grinds I’ve ever done - and even tougher on someone lighter like Quentin, who was carrying a heavier pack-to-bodyweight ratio. Even though I waited up and shared my water to offer some help, my urgency and matter-of-fact attitude came off as indifferent. Talking it out a few days later made me realize how insensitive and unencouraging my attitude had unintentionally come across - which was a fair point I’ll be taking note of going forward.

We eventually made it to our hostel at upper Yubeng around 2030. Check in took a while. The host was kinda annoyed we weren’t able to scan some QR code they provided us as he thought we were locals. Eventually we had to use our passport as identification. After putting our things down, we had dinner at the restaurant just below our room. The payment counter was the same as the check-in counter. And there were cats.

![](media/day-05/dinner.webp)

We stayed here all 3 nights in Yubeng. From what I heard, some local hikers will find lodging when they arrive at Yubeng, so they have the freedom to change lodging. The bath and face towels were one time use, and I had to use the face towel as replacement for the bath towel for the second day as it wasn't restocked. We requested extras on the third day. Honestly for the price I can't really complain, though I have nothing to compare to.
