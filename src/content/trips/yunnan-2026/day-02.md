Early in the morning, right after we turned out of the alley of our stay, we were approached by a random elderly woman who started advertising for a tour package that was too good to be true. Initial price was 268 RMB, 68 was advertised. After some questioning if it was a legitimate service, we followed her hesitantly to an actual physical shop. I asked our hotel owner if he knew of this company but he unfortunately didn't. Nevertheless, we ended up signing up for the tour which promised us a round trip around Erhai Lake (洱海).

First stop of the tour was 桃花岛 (Peach Island). It was a small place off track of the main road, near to the perimeter of Erhai Lake.

After returning the bikes, we had 过桥米线 (crossing the bridge rice noodles), packaged with the tour, and then took a short ride on a small boat around the moat of the island and pulled ashore to a tea shop where we tried some of their local tea. We bought some rock hard tea balls as a souvenir.

Our second stop was Xizhou Ancient Town (喜洲古镇). Like any other old town in Yunnan, it was lined with shops selling local specialties such as wild mushrooms and rose flower cake (鲜花饼), among other snacks and souvenirs, which we both tried.

Next, we went to a place where we were given a chance to pay to ride a horse up a small hill. Both me and Quentin took the offer. At the top, we sat around a miniature amphitheatre where a woman started to blow on a whistle. A few moments later, a group of peacocks came flying from the back to in front of us. Did not expect that.

![](media/day-02/32-peacock.webp)
![](media/day-02/33-sitting-peacock.webp)

Afterwards we followed a small path towards a terrace that sold local snacks and coffee. We sat around to rest and were given some complimentary snacks to chew on.

![](media/day-02/35-snacks.webp)

Our next stop was Shuanglang Ancient Town (双廊古镇). Nothing much to elaborate here, the vibe and aesthetic was similar to the other ancient towns mentioned previously. It really shows the amount of commercialisation that has taken place over the years due to tourism. We bought a cup of pomegranate juice to share, which Quentin quite liked, given it was his first time trying. We took some dog pictures as well.

Our final stop was the Santorini of Dali. It is a Greek-inspired resort designed to mimic the look of Santorini, Greece. The tour bus dropped us off nearby. I didn’t think it was planned for us to be given enough time to climb up the resort but we did it anyway. It was especially tiring given the high altitude. We realised we could have taken a shorter path when heading back. With little time we had, we quickly took scenic pictures and left. We totally didn't have to waste our energy like that but when would we ever go there again?

Back down, Quentin got a sausage as a snack since it was nearing evening and we were pretty hungry.

Coincidentally, around five to ten minutes before leaving, there was some singing and celebration going on. According to the banner in the video I took, its called 烟火海东天天火把节 (Haidong Daily Fireworks Festival). Apparently it's supposed to be celebrated on the 25th day of the 6th lunar month, 28th July 2026, but I guess this place is special.

![](media/day-02/51-singing.mp4)

Anyway, our tour ended around 8-9pm. We decided to head to Dali Ancient Town (大理古镇) to have our dinner, since our stay was nearby.

It took some time for us to decide but we ended up searching for a popular spot and had wild mushroom hotpot. The mushrooms had to be cooked in the hotpot for at least 25 minutes before we can start eating (The waitress told us that theres small worms in the mushrooms that needed to be killed).

Oh and we ended up convincing ourselves into buying a container of 鲜花饼 as snacks during our hike.
